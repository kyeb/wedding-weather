Temperatures in °F. Averages from Environment and Climate Change Canada daily records, FERNIE station (climate ID 1152850, elev. 3,284 ft), 1913–2026. Each weekend uses a 7-day window centered on it (Wednesday–Tuesday, so windows tile the year without overlap), pooling ~730 historical day-records. “Rainy days” is the share of those days with measurable rain — read it as a relative comparison between weekends. The lodge sits ~1,170 ft higher than the station, so expect it to run ~4 °F cooler on a standard lapse rate (its microclimate bends this). Winter weekends see snow far more often than rain (snowy-day odds are in each tooltip). Smoke: “smoky day” means a human observer at Cranbrook A airport (60 mi west, same airshed) logged Smoke in the hourly weather record — a consistent instrument from 1968 through today. Bars show 2015–2025, the modern fire era; small ticks show the 1968–2014 baseline, when smoke touched fewer than 2% of days in every week of the year. NOAA satellite smoke-plume maps (2005–2025) and Elk Valley PM2.5 monitors (2018–2025) independently confirm the seasonal shape; the tooltip includes the share of days with a heavy plume overhead. The step change between eras is the signal: smoke risk in Fernie is now ~10× what it was for most of the historical record.
